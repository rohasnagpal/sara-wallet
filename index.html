Match your own transfers
When you move money between your own wallets, or swap one token for another, Sara records it as two entries: one going out and one coming in. Press this button to pair them up, so moving your own money around isn't counted as money earned or spent. Press it after you've made transfers or swaps.
What you paid for your tokens
To work out profit or loss, Sara needs to know what you originally paid for the tokens you have. Pick a token and network, then press Rebuild. Sara reads through your history and lists what you still hold and what it cost you. Press Rebuild again whenever you have new transactions. For information only, not tax advice.
Profit & loss on a token
Shows how much you've gained or lost on one token. The first figure is for tokens you've already sent or sold, and the second is for tokens you still hold, at today's price. The oldest tokens are counted as sold first. Do the "What you paid" step above first, and leave the dates empty to include everything.
Money in and money out
Adds up the money that came in and the money that went out, in US dollars, across all your wallets. "Left over" is money in minus money out. Sara works out most transactions by itself (for example invoice payments count as money in, and payments you send count as money out), and moving money between your own wallets isn't counted. If something you received isn't counted, open it in the Ledger tab, press Edit, and choose what it counts as. Leave the dates empty to include everything.
Check your records
Looks for problems that could make the numbers above wrong, such as transactions with no dollar value, transactions Sara can't tell are money in or out, or the same transaction listed twice. Worth a check before you rely on the reports.
Download your records
Downloads every transaction as a spreadsheet you can share with your accountant, including amounts, dollar values, fees and labels. Choose Excel, or CSV if you want to open it in Google Sheets or other tools.

Compare stablecoin routes
Shows the ways to move a stablecoin from one network to another (or swap it on one network), with what you would receive after fees and gas, so you can pick the cheapest. Nothing is sent from here. These are only quotes.
USDC yield (Aave)
Supply USDC to Aave's v3 lending pool and earn its variable supply rate - your USDC stays withdrawable at any time. Sara only ever supplies or withdraws your own funds; it never borrows against them. Supported on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism and Polygon.
Pick a wallet to see your position.
Native USDC bridge (CCTP)
Moves USDC between networks via Circle's own Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol: real USDC is burned on the source network and newly minted on the destination - no wrapped token, no liquidity pool, no slippage. This needs two signed transactions (one per network), so your wallet needs a small amount of the destination network's native gas token too, not just the source one. For anything CCTP doesn't cover (other tokens, or a swap as part of the route), use Compare stablecoin routes above instead.

Pay & fetch (x402)
x402 is an open standard that lets an API charge per request over plain HTTP. Give Sara a URL - if it answers "402 Payment Required," Sara pays the exact USDC price from the wallet below and fetches the resource, no account or API key needed on either side. Set a spending policy scoped to this wallet/network (Business → Policies) to let payments under your cap go through automatically; without one, Sara asks for your passphrase before every payment, same as any other send. New to this? Pick Base Sepolia (testnet) first - it's free (faucet USDC, no real money) and isn't recorded in your ledger, so it's a safe way to try the whole flow. Also supported for real payments: Base, Polygon, Ethereum, Arbitrum.

Get paid: paywall generator
Pick a Sara wallet and a price, and Sara writes a single PHP file for you to paste at the top of any page on your own site. Once uploaded, that page charges the price below per visit before showing its content - no plugin, database, or special server needed (just PHP's standard curl and sodium extensions, present on virtually every host). Sara never sees traffic to your page; it only generates this code, once. Test mode runs on Base Sepolia (testnet, free faucet USDC) with no signup, so you can try the whole flow for free. Live mode settles real USDC via one of two facilitators: Circle's Gateway (Base only, completely free, no signup or API key at all) or Coinbase's CDP (Base, Polygon or Arbitrum, needs your own free CDP API key - Sara encrypts that secret the same way it protects a wallet's private key).
Get these from portal.cdp.coinbase.com → API Keys (free). When creating the key, choose the Ed25519 key type - this generator only supports Ed25519, not EC/ES256.

Wallet safety
When you swap or bridge USDC, Sara first gives its swap or bridge partner permission to spend that exact amount. The swap normally uses that permission up straight away. This checks whether any of Sara's own permissions are still open on a wallet, for example after a swap that failed, so you can cancel them. It doesn't cover approvals you gave in other apps or websites.
